Features that get you contacted

1. Fast, mobile-first pages

Most homeowners search for flooring contractors on their phones, often while standing in the room they want redone. If your pages are slow or the layout jumps around as images load, they leave. A good target is a score of 90 or higher on Google PageSpeed Insights on mobile and desktop. Large gallery photos are the usual culprit, so images need to be compressed and sized properly. Included: every Flooring WebPros plan, starting with Launch at 60/month, includes a 90+ Google PageSpeed score on desktop and mobile.

2. A short estimate request form

Ask for what you need to call back and nothing more: name, phone, ZIP code, flooring type and a rough room size. Every extra field gives people a reason to stop. Put the form, or a button to it, on every page. Included: an online estimate request form comes with every plan.

3. Click-to-call, WhatsApp and text buttons

Plenty of homeowners would rather call or text than fill in a form. A tappable phone number in the header, and a text or WhatsApp button where your customers use it, removes the friction. Keep these visible as people scroll on mobile. Included: click-to-call and text-us buttons are part of our lead capture setup; ask us if you want WhatsApp too.

4. Instant lead alerts by text and email

A lead that sits in an inbox until evening is a lead a faster competitor may already have called. Form submissions should reach your phone the moment they arrive. Included: on every Flooring WebPros site, leads arrive instantly by text and email.

Features that help you get found

5. A page for each service

A single "Services" page can't match searches for hardwood installation, LVP, tile, carpet and refinishing equally well. Give each service its own page explaining what you do, the materials you work with, your process and the questions people ask. Included: we build service pages and write the copy for you.

6. Service-area pages that are genuinely local

If you work across several towns, a page for each main area helps you show up in those searches. The key word is genuinely: each page needs real local content, such as jobs you've done there, photos and the neighborhoods you cover. Copying one page and swapping the city name rarely works. Our city-by-city local SEO strategy explains how to do this well. Included: local SEO is part of the Build plan (80/month) and Scale plan (120/month).

7. LocalBusiness, Service and FAQ schema markup

Schema markup is code that tells search engines, in a structured way, what your business is, what services you offer and which questions your page answers. It's documented at schema.org and is invisible to visitors. It doesn't guarantee rankings, but it helps search engines and AI tools understand your site correctly. Included: schema markup and on-page SEO come on every site.

8. An llms.txt file and AI-crawler access (optional)

More homeowners now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews for recommendations. Two small steps can help AI tools read your site: not blocking AI crawlers in your robots.txt file, and adding an llms.txt file, a plain text summary of your business and key pages. llms.txt is an optional, emerging convention rather than an official standard, so treat it as a low-effort extra, not a magic switch. See our post on getting recommended by AI search. Included: AI search visibility optimization is part of Build and Scale.

9. A blog that answers real questions

Homeowners search for things like "how long does hardwood refinishing take" or "is LVP waterproof". A few clear, helpful posts that answer those questions give search engines and AI tools more reasons to show your business, and give you links to send customers. Quality beats volume. Included: not part of the standard plans, so ask us if you want help with it.

Features that build trust

10. A project gallery with before/after sliders

Flooring is visual, and real photos of your own work are the strongest proof you have. A before/after slider lets visitors drag across a single photo to see the change, which works especially well for refinishing and repairs. Label projects by service and area, and skip stock photos. Included: project galleries are part of our website builds.

11. Reviews shown on the site

Homeowners read reviews before they call. Showing a selection of genuine reviews on your homepage and service pages means visitors don't have to leave your site to find them. You can quote reviews with the customer's first name and the job type, or embed them with a review widget. Included: we add your reviews to the site by quoting or embedding them. Reviews aren't pulled in automatically, and our separate Review Generation add-on handles automated text and email review requests.

12. Clear pricing guidance or a cost estimator

"Call for a quote" with no other information makes people nervous. You don't have to publish exact prices, but explaining typical ranges, what affects the cost (subfloor prep, removal, stairs, materials) and what's included in your estimate helps the right customers contact you. Some businesses add a simple cost estimator; others keep it to clear written guidance. Included: we write pricing guidance into your copy based on what you're comfortable sharing.

13. A clear FAQ section

An FAQ section answers the questions that stop people calling: how long jobs take, whether you move furniture, how long before you can walk on the floor, whether you offer a guarantee. Short, direct answers also suit search engines and AI tools. Included: FAQs are part of our copywriting, with FAQ schema where it fits.

Features that depend on your business

14. Financing information, if you offer it

Whole-home installs can be a big expense, and some homeowners look for monthly payment options. If you offer financing through a lender, say so clearly and explain how to apply. If you don't, leave it out rather than hinting at it. Included: we can add a page or section explaining your financing options; we don't provide or integrate financing applications.

15. A room visualizer (optional, mostly for showrooms)

A room visualizer lets shoppers upload a photo of their room and preview different floors. It's most useful for showrooms and retailers selling many styles. Third-party tools such as Roomvo offer this and can often be added to a site. For most installers and refinishers, a strong gallery does more. Included: no, Flooring WebPros doesn't provide a room visualizer.

Bonus: accessibility basics

Your site should work for everyone, including older homeowners and people using screen readers. The basics: readable text sizes, good color contrast, descriptive alt text on photos, form fields with labels, and buttons that work with a keyboard. These also tend to make the site easier for everyone to use, so check them on whatever platform you choose.

How to check your current flooring website in 15 minutes

You don't need an agency audit to see which of these features you're missing. Grab your phone and work through these steps as if you were a homeowner seeing your site for the first time.

  • Search for yourself. Search for your main service and city, such as "hardwood refinishing [your city]", and note whether your site or Google Business Profile appears at all.
  • Time the first load. Open your homepage on mobile data, not Wi-Fi. If you're waiting for photos to appear, run the page through PageSpeed Insights.
  • Try to contact yourself. Tap the phone number, then fill in the estimate form. Count the fields, check it works and see how long the lead takes to reach you.
  • Look for proof. Can you find real photos of your own jobs and a few genuine reviews within one or two scrolls?
  • Check your service pages. Does each service you want more of have its own page, with your areas mentioned naturally?
  • Read it like a stranger. Would someone know what you do, where you work and roughly what to expect on price within a few seconds?

Anything that fails is a good place to start. Fixing the contact path and speed usually comes first, because those affect every visitor. For more ideas, see our fixes for a flooring website that isn't getting leads.
