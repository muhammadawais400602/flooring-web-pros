Search "flooring installer near me" on your phone. Before any website shows up, Google shows a map with three local businesses, their star ratings, and a big "Call" button. That's the Map Pack, and for most flooring searches it's where homeowners decide who to call.

The good news: the Map Pack isn't won by the biggest company or the biggest ad budget. It's won by the business that gives Google the clearest, most trustworthy picture of who they are, what they do and where they work. Here's how to become that business.

How Google picks the three businesses

Google says local results are based on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your business matches the search. "Hardwood refinishing" needs to be something Google clearly knows you do.
  • Distance: how close you are to the person searching, or to the area they named.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted your business looks online: reviews, ratings, mentions on other sites and your website.

You can't move your shop, but you can control relevance and prominence almost completely. Every step below improves one of them.

Step 1: Set up your Google Business Profile properly

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in the Map Pack. Most flooring profiles we see are half-finished. Fill in everything:

  • Primary category: pick the one that matches your main money-maker, such as "Flooring contractor", "Flooring store" or "Floor refinishing service". Then add secondary categories for everything else you do.
  • Services: list each one separately: hardwood installation, LVP installation, tile installation, sanding and refinishing, carpet installation, epoxy coating. Each is a chance to match a search.
  • Service areas: if you go to customers' homes, add the cities and neighborhoods you actually serve. Don't list places you won't drive to.
  • Hours, phone and website: keep them accurate. Your business name, address and phone should match your website exactly.
  • Description: say in plain words what you do, where, and what makes you different. Write for homeowners, not for Google.

Don't stuff keywords into your business name. "Smith Flooring – Best Hardwood Installer Dallas" breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended. Use your real business name.

Step 2: Post real photos of real jobs, every month

Flooring is visual, and your profile should prove your work before anyone calls. Upload photos of finished jobs, before/after shots, your crew, your van and your showroom if you have one. Phone photos are fine: good light and a clean room matter more than the camera.

Keep adding them. A profile with fresh photos from last week looks like an active business. One with three photos from 2019 looks closed.

Step 3: Get reviews on a steady schedule

Reviews are the biggest prominence signal you control, and they're what homeowners read before they call. What matters:

  • Ask every happy customer, on the day you finish the job, while they're admiring their new floors. Send a text with your direct review link (you'll find it under "Ask for reviews" in your profile).
  • Steady beats sudden. Two reviews a week for a year looks far more natural than 40 in one week.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad. A calm, helpful reply to a bad review often wins you more trust than the review cost you.
  • Never buy reviews or offer discounts for them. It's against Google's rules, and fake reviews get removed.

If customers mention the service and the town in their own words ("They refinished our oak floors in Plano"), even better. You can't script it, but you can ask them to mention what you did.

Step 4: Back it up with your website

Google cross-checks your profile against your website. A strong website makes your profile more believable:

  • One page per service. A page about hardwood refinishing ranks for refinishing searches far better than a single "Services" page listing everything.
  • Service-area pages for the main towns you cover, each with genuinely useful local content: jobs you've done there, photos and the neighborhoods you work in. Copy-pasted pages with only the city name swapped don't help.
  • Your name, address and phone on every page, matching your profile exactly.
  • Fast and mobile-friendly. Most Map Pack clicks come from phones.

Step 5: Get listed on the sites that matter

Mentions of your business on other trusted sites (called citations) help Google confirm you're real and local. Start with the big ones: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, Nextdoor, Houzz and your local chamber of commerce. If you're certified by a manufacturer or a trade association, make sure you're in their directory too.

Keep your name, address and phone identical everywhere. Inconsistent details make Google less sure about you.

Step 6: Keep the profile active

Google Business Profile has a "Posts" feature, like a mini social media feed. Use it a few times a month: a finished job with photos, a seasonal offer, a tip about caring for new floors. Posts don't replace reviews, but an active profile signals an active business and gives searchers more reasons to pick you.

Mistakes that hold flooring businesses back

  • Using a P.O. box or virtual office as your address (against the rules and a common reason for suspensions).
  • Having two profiles for the same business, which splits your reviews and confuses Google.
  • Choosing a vague primary category like "Contractor" instead of a flooring category.
  • Ignoring the questions and reviews customers leave on your profile.
  • Changing your business name, phone or address without updating every listing.

How long does it take?

It depends on your city and how many competitors are doing this well. In smaller towns, a properly set-up profile with steady reviews can start showing in the Map Pack within weeks. In big, competitive metros it can take months of consistent work. Anyone who guarantees you the #1 spot is guessing, because nobody controls Google's results.

What you can guarantee is doing the work every month: photos, reviews, posts and a website that backs it all up. That's exactly what wins the Map Pack over time.
