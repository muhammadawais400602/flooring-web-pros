Google Ads puts your flooring business at the top of the page the moment a homeowner searches. Done well, it brings in ready-to-hire customers within days. Done badly, it quietly spends your money on clicks from DIYers and job seekers. This guide covers the setup that makes the difference.

When Google Ads makes sense for flooring

Google Ads tends to earn its keep when:

  • Your organic SEO is still ramping up
  • You've moved into a new area and need visibility now
  • You have specific capacity to fill, like a new crew or a slow season
  • Your typical job is large enough that one booked job covers a good chunk of your ad spend

It usually doesn't earn its keep when:

  • Your website doesn't convert visitors. Fix the site first, or you're paying to send people somewhere that loses them. See why flooring websites don't get leads.
  • Nobody can answer the phone or call back quickly.
  • Your jobs are small enough that ad costs eat most of the profit.

The keyword structure

Flooring ads work best organized into three groups of keywords. Put most of your budget on the first.

Group 1: High intent

Service plus location searches:

  • "hardwood installation [city]"
  • "carpet installer [city]"
  • "LVP installer near me"
  • "floor refinishing [city]"

These people want a specific service in a specific place. They're the closest to picking up the phone.

Group 2: Problem-based

  • "replace carpet with hardwood"
  • "refinish or replace hardwood"
  • "best flooring for basement"
  • "pet-friendly flooring installer"

These homeowners are still deciding. Send them to helpful landing pages that answer the question and end with an easy way to request an estimate.

Group 3: Your brand

  • "[your business name]"
  • "[your business name] reviews"

Bidding on your own name is usually inexpensive and stops competitors from appearing above you when someone searches for you specifically.

Keywords to never bid on

  • "flooring" on its own (too broad)
  • "cheap flooring" (often price shoppers looking for materials)
  • "flooring jobs" (job seekers, not customers)
  • "flooring DIY" (not your buyer)

Add these as negative keywords on day one, then check your search terms report every week and add new irrelevant searches as you find them.

Setting your budget

Click costs vary widely by city, service and how many competitors are bidding, so any national average would mislead you. Instead:

  1. Open Google Ads Keyword Planner and enter your main service and city keywords.
  2. Look at the estimated click costs for your area.
  3. Pick a monthly budget you can sustain for at least 60 to 90 days. Ads need time and data before you can judge them fairly.
  4. Start with your highest-intent keywords only, then expand once you know your cost per booked job.

Landing pages: the make-or-break

Sending ad clicks to your homepage makes the homeowner hunt for what they searched for. A page that matches their exact search answers it immediately, and usually turns far more of those clicks into calls.

Each ad group should have its own landing page with:

  • A headline that matches the search, like "Hardwood Floor Installation in Plano, TX"
  • A short estimate form near the top
  • A click-to-call button that's always in reach on mobile
  • A few honest trust signals: your star rating, years in business, licensed and insured
  • Before/after photos of real jobs
  • A realistic price range for your area, if you're comfortable sharing one
  • One clear call to action and no distracting navigation

Ad assets worth adding

Google Ads lets you add extra information to your ads, called assets (formerly extensions). They make your ad bigger and more useful at no extra cost to add:

  • Call assets: put a tappable phone number in the ad.
  • Location assets: show your address and distance, linked to your Google Business Profile.
  • Sitelink assets: link to pages like Services, Gallery or Reviews.
  • Callout assets: short phrases like "Licensed & insured" or "Free estimates."

Use every one that fits your business, and keep the wording accurate.

Run ads when you can answer the phone

A call nobody answers is money spent for nothing, and homeowners rarely leave voicemails for contractors they haven't met. Use Google Ads' ad schedule setting to run ads only during the hours someone can pick up, such as weekday business hours plus Saturday mornings. You can widen the schedule later if your form leads come in after hours and you call them back first thing.

Measuring what matters

Don't optimize for clicks. Optimize for cost per booked job.

Set up Google Ads conversion tracking for phone calls and form submissions so you can see which keywords and ads produce leads. Then keep a simple record of which leads became jobs, and calculate:

  • Cost per lead = ad spend ÷ leads
  • Lead to booked job rate = booked jobs ÷ leads
  • Cost per booked job = ad spend ÷ booked jobs
