Ask five web designers what a flooring website costs and you'll get five very different answers, from "free" to five figures. The price depends far less on the website itself than on who builds it, what's included and what happens after launch.

Here's an honest breakdown of your options, what each really costs over a year, and the questions to ask before you pay anyone.

DIY website builders

The appeal: low monthly cost and full control.

The real cost: your time. Writing every page, choosing photos, setting up forms, learning basic SEO and fixing things when they break can easily take dozens of hours. For a business owner who bills by the job, those are evenings and weekends not spent quoting or installing. DIY sites also tend to look like DIY sites, and homeowners spending thousands on floors notice.

Good fit if: you're just starting, money is very tight and you enjoy this kind of work.

Hiring a freelancer

The appeal: a custom site for less than an agency.

The real cost: quality and reliability vary enormously. Some freelancers are excellent. Others disappear after launch, leaving you with a site nobody can update. Ask who will host it, who fixes it when it breaks, and what changes cost after launch.

Good fit if: you know someone with a strong portfolio and a track record of sticking around.

Web design agencies

The appeal: a team, a process and usually a polished result.

The real cost: a large upfront invoice, timelines of 6–12 weeks, and SEO, content and ongoing changes often billed separately. Most generalist agencies also don't know flooring, so you'll spend time explaining the difference between LVP and engineered hardwood.

Good fit if: you're a larger multi-location business with a marketing budget and someone in-house to manage the project.

Monthly website plans

The appeal: no big upfront cost. The design, hosting, maintenance and often SEO are bundled into one predictable monthly fee.

The real cost: you're paying for a service, not buying an asset. Read the fine print on what happens if you cancel. With most plans, including ours, the website stays with the provider and you keep your domain.

Good fit if: you want a professional site live quickly, don't want to manage it yourself, and would rather spend money on getting found than on a one-time build.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Hosting and SSL: is it included, and who manages it?
  • Changes after launch: can you get new photos or a price update added without a new invoice?
  • Copywriting: who writes the words? Many builds assume you'll supply all the text.
  • SEO: is the site just "SEO-friendly", or will someone actually work on your rankings and Google Business Profile?
  • Domain: who owns it? Make sure the domain is registered in your name.
  • Contract length: some plans lock you in for 12 months or more.

What a flooring website should include, whatever you pay

  • A mobile-first design that loads fast on a phone.
  • A clear estimate request form and tap-to-contact buttons on every page.
  • A project gallery with before/after photos of your own jobs.
  • A separate page for each service you offer.
  • Your service areas, reviews and contact details easy to find.
  • Basic on-page SEO, so Google understands what you do and where.

If a quote doesn't include these, it's not really cheaper. You'll pay later in missed leads.
