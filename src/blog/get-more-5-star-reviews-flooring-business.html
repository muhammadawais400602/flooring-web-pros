Most happy flooring customers never leave a review. Not because they didn't love their floors, but because nobody asked at the right moment, or asking meant searching for your business and figuring out where to click.

The fix is a simple, repeatable system: ask every customer, at the right time, with a link that takes one tap. Here's how to set it up, including templates you can copy.

Why reviews matter so much for flooring

Reviews affect almost every step of how a homeowner finds and picks a flooring contractor:

  • Google Map Pack: Google lists review count and rating among the signals of how prominent a local business is.
  • AI search: tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI answers draw on business listings and reviews when recommending local companies. More in our AI search guide.
  • Clicks: a strong star rating next to your name makes homeowners more likely to choose you from the list.
  • Trust: flooring is a big purchase in someone's home. Reviews from neighbors are what reassure them.

Recency matters too. A steady stream of recent reviews tells homeowners you're active and doing good work now, not just years ago.

The automated review request system

The contractors with the most reviews don't rely on remembering to ask. They make it automatic.

Step 1: Trigger it when the job is done

Whatever you use to track jobs, whether it's job management software or a spreadsheet, marking a job complete should be the moment the request goes out.

Step 2: Get your one-tap review link

Your Google Business Profile gives you a direct link to your review form (look for "Ask for reviews" or "Share review form" in your profile). One tap takes the customer straight to the stars, with no searching or scrolling.

Step 3: Text soon after the job is finished

Send it the same day, while the floors are fresh and the customer is still admiring them:

Hi [First Name]! It was a pleasure finishing your [floor type] today. Would you take 30 seconds to leave us a quick Google review? Here's the link: [your review link]. Thank you! [Your Name], [Business]

Step 4: Email a few days later if there's no review

Subject: One quick favor, [First Name]

Thanks again for choosing [Business] for your [floor type] project. If you have 30 seconds, a quick Google review helps us a lot, and helps other homeowners find a contractor they can trust.

[Your review link]

Thanks either way!
[Your Name]

Step 5: One last friendly text about a week after the job

Hi [First Name]! Just a friendly nudge in case our review link got buried. If you have a minute: [link]. If not, no worries at all. Enjoy the new floors!

Then stop. Three touches is plenty. More than that starts to feel like pestering.

Ask everyone, not just the customers you think are happy. Sending only satisfied customers to Google and steering unhappy ones elsewhere is called review gating, and Google's policies prohibit it. The same goes for paying for reviews or offering discounts in exchange.

What earns a 5-star review in the first place

Automation only helps if the job deserves the review. These habits are what customers end up writing about:

Set expectations in writing

Send a text the night before: "We'll be there between 8 and 8:30am tomorrow. Expect some dust around the work area. We'll clean up fully before we leave."

Clean up better than they expect

Dust and mess are a common complaint about flooring work. Over-deliver on cleanup, and leave the room looking ready to live in.

Walk through the finished job

Before you leave, spend a few minutes walking the customer through the work: what you did, what to watch for in the first few days, how to care for the floor, and the number to call if anything comes up.

Check in after a couple of weeks

A short text, "How are the floors treating you?", shows you care after you've been paid. If they haven't left a review yet and they're happy, it's a natural moment to share the link again.

How to handle a 1-star review

It happens to every contractor eventually. How you respond matters more than the review itself.

Respond publicly and calmly, within a day or so

Hi [Name], I'm sorry we fell short of what you expected. I'd like to make this right. Please call me directly at [owner's phone]. [Owner First Name], Owner

Future customers read your replies. A calm, helpful response shows them how you handle problems, and that's often what they really want to know.

Then actually make it right

If the complaint is fair, offer a specific fix: a re-coat, a repair, a partial refund. If it isn't, stay polite, stick to the facts in your reply and move on. Never argue in public.

Ask for an update

If you resolve the problem, it's fine to politely ask the customer whether they'd consider updating their review. Don't pressure them or offer anything in return.

Showing reviews on your website

Reviews belong on your website too, where homeowners are deciding whether to call:

  • Your overall star rating and review count near the top of the homepage
  • A few of your best reviews on the homepage
  • Reviews that mention a specific service on that service's page
  • Reviews from a specific town on that town's page
