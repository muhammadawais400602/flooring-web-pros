For a flooring contractor, your Google Business Profile is the listing that shows up in Google Maps and the Map Pack: the three businesses on the map at the top of searches like "hardwood floor installation near me." Those three spots get a big share of the clicks, and many homeowners call straight from them without ever visiting a website.

That makes your profile at least as important as your website. Here's how to set up every part of it.

Step 1: Pick the right primary category

Your primary category tells Google what kind of business you are and which searches you fit. Choose the one that matches your main source of work: Flooring contractor for most installers, Flooring store if you mainly sell from a showroom, or Floor refinishing service if refinishing is your core business. Avoid vague choices like "General contractor" or "Home improvement."

Then add secondary categories for everything else you genuinely do. Depending on your business, options include:

  • Carpet installer
  • Tile contractor
  • Floor refinishing service
  • Flooring store or Carpet store (if you have a showroom)

Category names change from time to time, so type a few words into the category box and see what Google offers. Only add categories that truly apply.

Step 2: List every service individually

Don't lump everything into "flooring." Add each service as its own item:

  • Hardwood installation
  • Hardwood refinishing
  • Engineered hardwood installation
  • Carpet installation
  • LVP / vinyl plank installation
  • Tile and stone installation
  • Laminate installation
  • Epoxy flooring
  • Commercial flooring
  • Floor repair

Each service tells Google and homeowners exactly what you handle, which helps your profile match more specific searches.

Step 3: Set service areas that match where you work

If you go to customers' homes, you're a service-area business. Google lets you add up to 20 service areas, as cities, ZIP codes or wider regions. Add the places you actually serve and leave out the ones you won't drive to. Listing towns two hours away just brings in calls you'll turn down.

If customers don't visit your address, hide it. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their location shouldn't show that address.

Step 4: Add photos regularly, not when you remember

Flooring sells on looks, and an active profile with recent photos looks like an active business. Set a weekly reminder and upload a few photos from that week's jobs. Phone photos are fine; good light and a clean room matter more than the camera.

Photos worth adding:

  • Before/after shots of completed jobs
  • Your crew on-site
  • Your showroom or shop exterior
  • Products you carry
  • Wide shots of finished rooms

Step 5: Post a few times a month

Google Posts are short updates that appear on your profile. Use them for:

  • Current offers
  • New products in your showroom
  • Recent jobs, with photos
  • Seasonal tips, like letting hardwood acclimate before install

Posts don't replace reviews, but they show searchers you're busy and give them more reasons to pick you. Our Scale plan includes 4 Google Business Profile posts a month, done for you.

Step 6: Get reviews the right way

Reviews are what homeowners read before they call, and recent ones count for a lot with them. A steady flow of new reviews looks far better than a pile from years ago and nothing since.

  • Ask every happy customer, ideally the day you finish, by text or email with your direct review link. You'll find it under "Ask for reviews" in your profile.
  • Reply to every review, including the five-star ones. A calm, helpful reply to a bad review often earns more trust than the review cost you.
  • Never buy reviews or offer discounts for them. It's against Google's rules, and fake or incentivised reviews can be removed.

Step 7: Keep your name, address and phone consistent

Your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP) should match exactly across:

  • Your website
  • Your Google Business Profile
  • Yelp, Houzz, Angi, the Better Business Bureau and similar directories
  • Facebook and Instagram
  • Chamber of commerce and trade association listings

"Smith Flooring LLC" in one place, "Smith Flooring" in another and "Smith Floors" in a third makes it harder for Google to be sure they're all the same business. Pick one version and use it everywhere.

Common Google Business Profile mistakes

  • Keyword stuffing the business name ("Smith Flooring - Dallas Hardwood Installation"), which breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended
  • Using a P.O. box or virtual office as your address, which isn't allowed
  • Showing your address when customers don't come to you
  • Having two profiles for the same business, which splits your reviews
  • Letting the profile go stale for months, with no new photos, posts or review replies
