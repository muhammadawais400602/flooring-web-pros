What a flooring website platform actually needs to do

Before comparing platforms, it helps to be clear on the job. A flooring company website needs to load quickly on a phone, show real project photos without slowing down, make it easy to call or request an estimate, and give you a clear page for each service and main area you cover. Our checklist of flooring website features goes through these in detail. Any of the platforms below can do most of this. The difference is how much work it takes you to get there and keep it there.

WordPress

WordPress is one of the most widely used website platforms and the default choice for many web designers. It's open source, so you choose your own hosting, theme and plugins.

Pros: almost unlimited flexibility, a huge plugin library for forms, galleries and SEO, full control over pages and code, and plenty of developers who know it.

Cons: everything is your responsibility, including core, theme and plugin updates, security, backups and speed. Sites often slow down as plugins pile up, and a bad update can break a page. Page builders make editing easier but can add weight.

Good fit if: you have a developer or agency you trust to maintain it, or you enjoy the technical side yourself.

Wix

Wix is a hosted drag-and-drop website builder. You pick a template, move things around and publish.

Pros: quick to start, no hosting to manage, lots of design freedom and an app market for bookings, forms and reviews widgets.

Cons: it's easy to build something that looks fine on a desktop but feels heavy or cluttered on a phone. Apps can add ongoing costs. And the builder doesn't write your service pages or do your local SEO; that part is still on you.

Good fit if: you want a simple site you can change yourself and have the time to learn the editor.

Squarespace

Squarespace is a hosted builder known for clean, design-focused templates.

Pros: polished templates that look good with big project photos, a tidy editor, and hosting and security handled for you.

Cons: less layout freedom than Wix and fewer integrations than WordPress. Templates are often built for portfolios and small shops rather than lead-focused local service businesses, so you may need to rework them to put the estimate form and phone number front and center.

Good fit if: you have strong photos, want a clean look and are happy keeping the site fairly simple.

Shopify

Shopify is an ecommerce platform built for selling products online, with a content system attached.

Pros: excellent for product catalogs, checkout, payments, stock and shipping. If you sell flooring, underlayment, trim or care products online, it's hard to beat.

Cons: it's built around products, not estimate requests. Service businesses end up fighting the store structure, and the app costs and transaction fees don't buy you much if you don't sell online.

Good fit if: you run a flooring store that ships or sells products online. Most installers and refinishers don't need it.

Webflow

Webflow is a visual builder aimed at designers, with fine control over layout and clean output.

Pros: fast, well-structured pages when built well, strong design control, built-in hosting and good SEO settings. Content editors can update text and blog posts without touching the design.

Cons: the learning curve is steep for a busy contractor. Most businesses need a designer to build it and often to change layouts later, so you're paying for the platform and for someone's time.

Good fit if: you have a designer or agency that works in Webflow and you care a lot about a distinctive look.

Questions to ask before you pick a platform

Whichever way you lean, these questions will save you from the most common regrets:

  • Who will make changes, and how fast? If adding a new service page or gallery photos means waiting weeks or doing it yourself at night, it probably won't happen.
  • Who handles updates and security? On a self-managed platform, that's you or your developer. On a hosted builder or managed site, it's mostly handled for you.
  • Who owns the domain and content? Make sure the domain is registered in your business's name and that you can get your text and photos if you leave.
  • How will leads reach you? Check where estimate requests go and whether you're alerted straight away on your phone.
  • What will it really cost each month? Add up the plan, apps or plugins, premium themes and anyone's time, not just the headline plan.
  • Can it support local SEO? You'll want control over page titles, descriptions, headings, schema markup and a page for each service and main area. Our local SEO strategy for flooring installers shows why that matters.

If you're switching platforms

Moving from one platform to another is common, and it doesn't have to cost you your rankings. The most important step is mapping every old page address to its new one and setting up redirects, so search engines and any old links land on the right page instead of an error. Keep your business name, address and phone number exactly the same, carry over your best content and photos, and resubmit your sitemap in Google Search Console after launch. Then keep an eye on any pages that stop getting visits for the first few weeks.

How to choose: simple decision rules

  • You sell flooring products online with checkout? Choose Shopify.
  • You have a developer you trust and want maximum flexibility? Choose WordPress.
  • You work with a designer and want a standout look? Choose Webflow.
  • You want to build and edit a simple site yourself? Choose Wix for layout freedom or Squarespace for polished templates.
  • You don't want to manage a website at all? Choose a done-for-you managed site.

The platform matters less than the content. A fast site with a clear page for each service, real job photos and an easy estimate form will beat a fancy platform with thin content on any of these options.
