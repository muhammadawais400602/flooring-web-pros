Where flooring website templates come from

Most flooring website templates come from one of three places: WordPress themes, website builders, or e-commerce themes. Each works differently:

  • WordPress themes. You buy or download a theme, install it on WordPress and pay for your own hosting. Some themes are sold for contractors or home services in general; very few are built specifically for flooring. You get lots of flexibility, but also updates, plugins and security to manage.
  • Website builders such as Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy. You pick a starter design inside the builder and edit it in your browser. Hosting is included in a monthly plan, and setup is easier, but you are tied to that platform's features and layouts.
  • Shopify themes. These are built for selling products online. They can suit a flooring retailer that sells materials or accessories online, but they are usually a poor fit for an installer whose real goal is estimate requests.

What a flooring template must include

A flooring template is only worth using if it can handle the pages and features a flooring business needs to win jobs. Before you buy, make sure it supports:

  • A page per service: separate, well-structured pages for hardwood, LVP, tile, carpet, refinishing or whatever you do. A single "Services" page with a bullet list will not rank for much.
  • Service-area content: room to name the towns you serve, ideally with a page for each main one.
  • Project galleries that load fast and can be organized by floor type, ideally with before/after sliders for refinishing work.
  • A short estimate request form that sends leads to your phone and email straight away.
  • Click-to-call that stays visible on mobile.
  • Clean headings and room for schema markup, so search engines can understand your business.
  • Easy editing for when you finish a job worth adding.

How to evaluate a template before you commit

The best way to evaluate a template is to test its live demo the way a homeowner and Google would. It takes about fifteen minutes and can save you weeks:

  1. Run the demo through PageSpeed Insights. Paste the demo URL into Google PageSpeed Insights and look at the mobile result first. If a demo with no real content is already slow, your finished site, with your photos and plugins, will usually be slower.
  2. Open it on your own phone. Can you find the phone number in one second? Is the menu easy to use with a thumb? Does text stay readable without zooming?
  3. Test the forms. Find out how form submissions are delivered, whether you get a text or only an email, and whether you need a paid plugin or plan for that.
  4. Check the page types. Does it include service pages, a gallery and a contact page, or only a homepage and a blog?
  5. Imagine it with your photos. Demos are styled around perfect stock images. Picture the layout with your own phone photos of real jobs.
  6. Check updates and support. For WordPress themes, look at when it was last updated and how support works. For builders, check which features require a higher plan.

Watch for "free" templates that are not really free. The theme may cost nothing, but the gallery, form or speed features you need often sit behind paid plugins or upgraded plans.

If you choose a template, how to make it work

If you go with a template, the content you put into it matters far more than the design you start from. A few habits make a template site much more useful:

  • Replace every stock photo with photos of your own jobs, crew and van. Homeowners can tell the difference.
  • Write a separate page for each service and name the towns you work in, instead of one generic "Services" page.
  • Keep the homepage simple: what you do, where, and how to get a price, with your phone number visible at the top.
  • Compress images before you upload them, and re-run PageSpeed Insights after the site is finished, not just on the demo.
  • Send a test enquiry through your own form every few weeks to make sure leads still arrive.
  • Connect it to your Google Business Profile and keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere. Our Google Business Profile guide for flooring contractors walks through it.

Template vs. custom: when each makes sense

A template makes sense when budget is the main constraint and you have time to do the work; custom makes sense when ranking and leads matter. Here is a simple way to decide:

SituationBetter fit
Brand-new business, almost no budget, you enjoy DIYTemplate
You just need a basic online presence for referralsTemplate
You want to rank for several services across several townsCustom
You have no time to write copy or build pagesCustom
Your current template site gets visits but few callsCustom
You run a showroom, multiple crews or commercial workCustom
