Flooring contractor website design is different from designing a site for a flooring store. Installers usually do not have a showroom, they work across several towns and they sell their skill and reliability, not a product catalogue. Your website has to do the selling you cannot do while you are on your knees laying plank: show the work, explain the services, cover the area and make it easy to ask for a price.

Flooring WebPros builds websites only for flooring businesses, installers included. Here is how we approach a site for an installation business, whether you work alone or run several crews.

What installers need from a website

Installers need a website that proves workmanship, covers their service area and captures leads while they are working. That comes down to a few essentials:

  • Proof of work: real photos of real jobs. Clean transitions, tight tile lines, stair nosings and finished rooms say more than any slogan.
  • A clear service list: hardwood, LVP and laminate, tile, carpet, sanding and refinishing, subfloor prep, stairs. Each service you want more of gets its own page.
  • Service areas: the towns and neighborhoods you actually drive to, so homeowners know you cover them and Google does too.
  • Fast contact: click-to-call, an online estimate request form and an optional WhatsApp button.
  • Instant lead alerts: estimate requests arrive by text and email, so you can call back between jobs.

The site grows with your business: a solo installer needs a lean, personal site, while a multi-crew company needs more service and area pages. Here is how we plan each:

What goes on each installer service page

Each service page answers the questions a homeowner has before they call about that one job. A page for LVP installation, for example, covers what you install, where you work, how the job runs and how to get a price. Every service page we write for an installer includes:

  • A plain-English opening that says what the service is and which towns you do it in.
  • What the job involves: removal of old flooring, subfloor checks and prep, installation, trim and transitions, and clean-up.
  • Photos of that service only, so a homeowner looking for tile is not scrolling past carpet.
  • Common questions answered in your words, such as how long the job takes or whether furniture needs to be moved.
  • A clear call to action with click-to-call and the estimate form on the same screen.

We write all of this copy for you from a short conversation about how you work. That is also what helps each page show up in search and in AI answers, because it describes your service clearly instead of repeating a slogan.

Making your job photos do the selling

Your job photos are your best sales tool, so we design the site around them. Galleries are organized by floor type, so a homeowner who wants tile sees tile. Refinishing and repair jobs can use before/after sliders that show the change in one swipe.

You do not need a professional photographer. Clear phone photos of finished rooms, taken in good daylight, work well. Send new ones any time and we add them as part of ongoing maintenance.
